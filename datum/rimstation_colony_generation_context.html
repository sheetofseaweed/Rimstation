rimstation_colony_generation_context
/datum/rimstation_colony_generation_context

The deterministic plan behind one generated colony landscape.

Z2 and Z3 read the same arrays: terrain on the upper level is therefore always the roof of the matching mountain footprint below. Coordinates are local to these bounds, so a future overmap cell can reuse this context without its result depending on a turf reservation's absolute world coordinates or load order. cell_identity already namespaces every stream when supplied. cell_profile is retained as the extension point for chosen-cell climate and geology; the planet remains the current source of those values.

proc
build_landing_distance_field
()

Measures outward from every authored landing turf instead of from its bounding box.

The radius is deliberately small, so stamping exact Euclidean distances around the authored tiles is both cheaper and more faithful than a whole-map path search. A round or irregular landing area therefore keeps a round or irregular transition instead of acquiring square corners from its extrema.

proc
is_sinkhole_site
(x, y, radius, /list/reserved_indices)

TRUE when a collapse centred here would sit in open country.

Sweeps the mouth plus its clearance ring and requires every tile of it to be plain lowland. Mountains, rivers, cave mouths, plateau stairs and the landing clearing all sit directly against the ground they belong to, so a hole reaching any of them would either swallow a route terrain had already placed or drain a river into the rock. Checking the whole footprint here is also what lets open_sinkhole() index without bounds checks.

proc
open_sinkhole
(centre_x, centre_y, radius)

Collapses one disc of lowland and excavates the underground under it.

The core always goes, so every mouth spans the same five tiles; the ring past it is rolled per tile, so no two are the same shape and none of them is a circle. The chamber below reaches wider than the mouth on purpose - a drop into a hole exactly the width of the hole lands in sealed rock, which is a trap rather than a route down.

select_raid_insertions
()

Picks two reachable proposals along each edge.

The raid system still validates real turf density and connectivity when an attack begins. These generated landmarks make that validation useful on a procedural map instead of asking one stale mapped coordinate to survive every possible mountain layout.

proc
select_sinkhole_exit
(/list/collapsed)

Picks the one tile of a collapse that can be climbed back out of.

Stairs lift a climber to the tile one step past them, so the climb has to start on the rim and face out of the hole: begun anywhere else, the top of the stair is more open air and the climber drops straight back in. Without this a sinkhole is a one-way fall, which is a hazard rather than the route down it is meant to be.

proc
select_sinkholes
()

Chooses the few places the surface has fallen into the level below it.

Two passes over the plan. The first takes only the sites a roll picks out, which is what scatters them instead of filling whichever corner the scan reaches first; the second runs only when that came up short and takes any site that fits, so a map whose rolls were unkind still has a way down rather than none.

Both passes do the cheap tests before is_sinkhole_site(), which sweeps fifty-odd tiles per candidate and would otherwise run on every coordinate of the map.
